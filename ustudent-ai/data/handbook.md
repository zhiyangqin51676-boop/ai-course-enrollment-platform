# U+ University — Undergraduate Student Handbook

> Edition: Fall 2024. This handbook governs enrolment, grading, and academic standing for all undergraduate students. Keep it open during enrolment week.

---

## 1. Academic Calendar

The academic year is divided into two main semesters and an optional summer term.

| Term | Weeks | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fall semester | 15 teaching weeks + 1 exam week | Starts late August |
| Spring semester | 15 teaching weeks + 1 exam week | Starts mid January |
| Summer term | 8 teaching weeks | Optional; limited course list |

### Key enrolment dates each semester

- **Week 1 (Add period):** Students may add any course they meet the prerequisites for, subject to seat availability.
- **End of Week 2 (Add/drop deadline):** Final day to add a course. Final day to drop a course with a full refund and no academic record.
- **End of Week 8 (Withdrawal deadline):** Final day to withdraw from a course. Withdrawn courses appear on the transcript with a grade of "W" but do not affect GPA. No refund after this date.
- **Week 15 (End of teaching):** No further enrolment changes.

## 2. Credits and Graduation Requirements

### Credit load

- Each course carries between 1 and 4 credits. The credit value reflects roughly the weekly contact + study hours.
- A student must enrol in **at least 12 credits** per semester to be considered full-time.
- A student may enrol in **at most 18 credits** per semester. To exceed 18, written approval from the Academic Advising Office is required and is normally granted only to students with a GPA of 3.5 or higher.

### Graduation

To graduate with a Bachelor's degree from U+ University, a student must:

1. Complete a minimum of **120 credits**.
2. Maintain a cumulative GPA of **2.0 or higher** at the time of graduation.
3. Complete all required courses for their declared major (see §6).
4. Complete the university-wide English Composition requirement (ENG101 or an approved equivalent).
5. Have no outstanding fees or academic integrity sanctions.

There is no minimum number of semesters required to graduate. Students who accumulate the required credits in fewer semesters (via summer terms or credit transfer) may graduate early upon application.

## 3. Enrolment Process

Enrolment is performed entirely through the ustudent portal. The recommended five-step process is:

1. **Browse the catalogue.** Use the course list page (filterable by department and credit value) to shortlist candidate courses.
2. **Check prerequisites.** Click each course to view its prerequisites. If you have not completed a prerequisite, the system will reject your enrolment attempt.
3. **Check for time conflicts.** The system will warn you if a candidate course overlaps with any course you are already enrolled in.
4. **Submit your enrolment.** Confirm the selection on the review page. Your seat is reserved at this point.
5. **Verify your schedule.** Open "My Courses" and confirm the new course appears with the correct section and time.

### Waitlists

When a course reaches its `max_students` cap, further enrolment attempts are placed on a waitlist. If a seat becomes available, the next student on the waitlist is enrolled automatically and notified by email. Students remain responsible for checking their email during enrolment week.

### Concurrent enrolment limits

A student may **not** be enrolled in two courses whose schedules overlap, even partially. This rule is enforced at submission time. To enrol in a course that conflicts with an existing one, you must drop the existing course first.

## 4. Prerequisites

A prerequisite is a course that must be completed (with a passing grade) before another course may be taken. The current prerequisites for the core curriculum are:

- **CS201 Data Structures and Algorithms** requires **CS101 Introduction to Computer Science**.
- **MATH201 Linear Algebra** requires **MATH101 Calculus I**.

In addition to course prerequisites, some courses carry **enrolment rules**:

- **CS201** also requires **sophomore standing or higher** and a minimum of **30 completed credit hours**.
- **MATH201** also requires a cumulative **GPA of 2.5 or higher**.

If a student believes they have completed an equivalent prerequisite elsewhere (e.g. transfer credit), they may file a prerequisite waiver request with the Academic Advising Office. Waivers are evaluated within ten working days.

## 6. Majors and Degree Requirements

Each undergraduate student declares a major by the end of their second semester. A major is a coherent program of study with a fixed list of required courses and elective groups.

- **Computer Science major.** Requires CS101, CS201, MATH101, MATH201, and a minimum of 30 additional credits in CS electives at the 300 level or higher.
- **Mathematics major.** Requires MATH101, MATH201, and a minimum of 36 additional credits in MATH electives at the 200 level or higher.
- **Undeclared.** Students who have not declared a major are limited to 100- and 200-level courses.

A student may change their major up to once per academic year by submitting a change-of-major form to the Academic Advising Office. Courses previously taken count toward the new major if they appear in the new major's required or elective list.

## 7. Grades, GPA, and Repeating Courses

### Grading scale

| Letter | Numeric range | Grade points |
|---|---|---|
| A | 90 – 100 | 4.0 |
| B | 80 – 89 | 3.0 |
| C | 70 – 79 | 2.0 |
| D | 60 – 69 | 1.0 |
| F | 0 – 59 | 0.0 |
| W | — (withdrawn) | not counted in GPA |
| I | — (incomplete) | converted to F if not resolved within one semester |

### GPA computation

GPA is the credit-weighted average of grade points across all graded courses. Formally:

```
GPA = sum(grade_points_i * credits_i for each course i)
      / sum(credits_i for each course i)
```

Withdrawn (W) and incomplete (I) courses are excluded from the denominator.

### Repeating a course

A student who receives a D or F grade may repeat the course once. The new grade replaces the old grade in the GPA calculation, but both attempts remain on the transcript. A student may not repeat a course more than once without written permission from the Dean.

## 8. Academic Standing

Academic standing is reviewed at the end of every semester.

- **Good standing:** cumulative GPA ≥ 2.0.
- **Academic warning:** cumulative GPA between 1.5 and 2.0 (inclusive of 1.5). The student must meet with an academic advisor before the next enrolment.
- **Academic probation:** cumulative GPA between 1.0 and 1.5. The student's credit load is capped at 13 credits for the next semester.
- **Academic suspension:** cumulative GPA below 1.0, or two consecutive semesters on probation. The student is suspended for one semester and must apply for reinstatement.

---

## 9. Refund Policy

- **Full refund** is granted for drops completed by the end of Week 2.
- **Partial refund (50%)** is granted for drops between the start of Week 3 and the end of Week 4.
- **No refund** is granted after the end of Week 4.
- Withdrawals (between Week 5 and Week 8) carry no refund but also do not affect GPA.

Refunds are issued to the original payment method within ten working days of the drop request.

### Privacy notice

The ustudent system stores the following personally identifiable information (PII): your full name, email, student ID, enrolment history, and grades. This information is retained for seven years after your last enrolled semester, after which it is anonymised for institutional research.

The system **does not** share your PII with third parties without your written consent, except where required by law. Faculty members may view enrolment and grade information for students enrolled in their courses; they may not view your wider transcript without your consent.

You may request a copy of all PII the system holds about you, or request its deletion, by emailing privacy@uplus.edu. Such requests are processed within thirty days.
