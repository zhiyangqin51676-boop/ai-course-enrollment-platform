## CS101 — Introduction to Computer Science

- **Credits:** 3
- **Max students:** 30
- **Instructor:** Dr. Wilson (wilson@university.edu)
- **Schedule:** Mon / Wed / Fri, 09:00 – 10:30
- **Prerequisites:** None
- **Enrolment rules:** None

### Description

A first course in computer science. Students learn the basic concepts of programming through structured problem solving in Python, including variables, conditionals, loops, functions, lists, and dictionaries. The course is designed for students with no prior programming experience.

### Topics covered

1. What a program is and how it runs
2. Variables, types, and arithmetic
3. Conditionals and Boolean logic
4. Loops and iteration
5. Functions and scope
6. Lists, tuples, and dictionaries
7. File I/O
8. A brief introduction to objects and classes

### Assessment

- Weekly homework assignments: 30 %
- Midterm exam: 30 %
- Final project (a small interactive program): 40 %

### Notes

This course satisfies the introductory computer science requirement for the Computer Science major and serves as the prerequisite for CS201.

## CS201 — Data Structures and Algorithms

- **Credits:** 4
- **Max students:** 25
- **Instructor:** Dr. Wilson (wilson@university.edu)
- **Schedule:** Tue / Thu, 11:00 – 12:30
- **Prerequisites:** CS101 (Introduction to Computer Science)
- **Enrolment rules:**
  - Sophomore standing or higher
  - At least 30 completed credit hours

### Description

A rigorous treatment of common data structures and the algorithms that operate on them. Students implement each structure from scratch in Python and analyse the running time and space cost of every operation. The course balances theoretical analysis with practical programming.

### Topics covered

1. Asymptotic analysis (big-O notation)
2. Arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues
3. Recursion and recurrence relations
4. Trees: binary search trees, AVL, heaps
5. Hash tables and hashing strategies
6. Graphs and graph traversal (BFS, DFS)
7. Sorting algorithms and their trade-offs
8. Dynamic programming, brief introduction

### Assessment

- Five programming assignments: 40 %
- Midterm exam: 20 %
- Final exam: 40 %

### Notes

CS201 is typically taken in the second semester of sophomore year. Students who took CS101 in their first semester and earned a B or higher are usually well prepared. Students who struggled in CS101 are strongly encouraged to attend the optional CS201 boot camp in Week 1.

## MATH101 — Calculus I

- **Credits:** 4
- **Max students:** 35
- **Instructor:** Dr. Brown (brown@university.edu)
- **Schedule:** Mon / Wed / Fri, 09:30 – 11:00
- **Prerequisites:** None
- **Enrolment rules:** None

### Description

A standard first-semester calculus course covering differential and integral calculus of a single variable. Students develop both computational fluency and conceptual understanding through worked examples, applications, and proofs.

### Topics covered

1. Limits and continuity
2. The derivative: definition and rules
3. Applications of differentiation (related rates, optimisation)
4. The definite integral and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
5. Techniques of integration (substitution, integration by parts)
6. Applications of integration (area, volume, work)

### Assessment

- Weekly problem sets: 25 %
- Two midterm exams: 35 %
- Final exam: 40 %

### Notes

This course conflicts with CS101 on the weekly schedule. A student cannot enrol in both in the same semester.

## MATH201 — Linear Algebra

- **Credits:** 3
- **Max students:** 25
- **Instructor:** Dr. Brown (brown@university.edu)
- **Schedule:** Tue / Thu, 14:00 – 15:30
- **Prerequisites:** MATH101 (Calculus I)
- **Enrolment rules:**
  - Cumulative GPA of 2.5 or higher

### Description

An introduction to vector spaces, linear maps, matrices, and their applications. The course emphasises both the algebraic manipulation of matrices and the geometric interpretation of linear transformations.

### Topics covered

1. Systems of linear equations and Gaussian elimination
2. Matrix algebra
3. Vector spaces, subspaces, basis, dimension
4. Linear transformations and matrix representations
5. Determinants
6. Eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and diagonalisation
7. Inner products and orthogonality (brief)

### Assessment

- Weekly problem sets: 30 %
- Midterm exam: 30 %
- Final exam: 40 %

### Notes

MATH201 is required for the Computer Science major and is recommended for students intending to take advanced courses in machine learning, computer graphics, or theoretical computer science.

## ENG101 — English Composition

- **Credits:** 3
- **Max students:** 20
- **Instructor:** Dr. Brown (brown@university.edu)
- **Schedule:** Mon / Wed / Fri, 13:00 – 14:30
- **Prerequisites:** None
- **Enrolment rules:** None

### Description

A required course in academic writing and communication. Students learn to plan, draft, and revise essays, technical reports, and short research papers. The course also covers source evaluation and citation conventions.

### Topics covered

1. Rhetorical situation: audience, purpose, context
2. The writing process: planning, drafting, revising
3. Argument and evidence
4. Source evaluation and integration
5. Citation conventions (APA, MLA, Chicago — overview)
6. Technical writing: clarity, concision, structure

### Assessment

- Five short essays: 50 %
- Research paper: 30 %
- In-class participation: 20 %

### Notes

ENG101 satisfies the university-wide English Composition requirement and is required for graduation in every undergraduate major.
